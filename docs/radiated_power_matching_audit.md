# Audit: radiated-power Gram → impedance → scattering pipeline

## Executive finding

The present implementation is internally useful as a **single-frequency,
passive, scalar surrogate**, but it is not yet a physical antenna-impedance
model. The broadside-heavy reflection is mostly the expected result of its
assumptions.

For a large regular planar array, the spatial Fourier transform implicit in
the current Gram gives, before grating lobes and edge corrections,

\[
R_\mathrm{active}(\theta,\phi)\ \propto\
\frac{D(\theta,\phi)}{\cos\theta}.
\]

Consequently, the implemented \(D(\theta)=2(n+1)\cos^n\theta\) gives

\[
R_\mathrm{active}(\theta)\ \propto\ \cos^{n-1}\theta.
\]

This means:

- hemispheric isotropic (\(n=0\)): active resistance tends to rise as
  \(1/\cos\theta\);
- \(n=1\): active resistance is approximately scan-independent;
- \(n>1\): active resistance tends to **fall**, not rise, away from broadside.

The \(1/\cos\theta\) factor is already generated by the angular-to-planar
spectral Jacobian. Multiplying the Gram by another projected-aperture factor
would double-count geometry. A decreasing projected aperture predicts reduced
gain, but does **not** by itself require antenna input impedance or reflection
to increase. TE and TM scan impedances can even move in opposite directions.

To obtain realistic scan mismatch, the model needs a physical, frequency-
dependent complex impedance (or an equivalent vector spectral-domain model),
an explicit fixed feed/matching network, and active-impedance diagnostics. It
should not be forced by an arbitrary scan-angle multiplier.

## Reproduction with the current implementation

An 8×8, \(d=0.5\lambda\) array was evaluated directly with the current SIMD
WASM. With the ad-hoc reactance disabled, the collective resistance
\(q^H Rq/q^Hq\) in ohms was:

- \(n=0\): 34.0 at 0°, 39.5 at 30°, 48.6 at 45°, 78.6 at 60°, 104.3 at 70°;
- \(n=1\): 63.6 at 0°, 63.0 at 30°, 62.3 at 45°, 60.3 at 60°, 58.6 at 70°;
- \(n=4\): 144.7 at 0°, 95.4 at 30°, 55.4 at 45°, 26.6 at 60°, 18.0 at 70°.

These finite-array values have edge ripple, but follow
\(\cos^{n-1}\theta\). With \(z_c=40+j4\ \Omega\),
\(X_\mathrm{nn}=10\ \Omega\), and \(\alpha=2\), the returned-power ratio
\(10\log_{10}(\lVert Sa\rVert^2/\lVert a\rVert^2)\) also has its largest
value at or near broadside for these examples. This is therefore not cured by
making the isolated element factor more directive; doing so strengthens the
model's broadside radiative coupling.

## What is sensible in the current framework

1. **The radiated-power Gram is formed on a proper solid-angle quadrature.**
   The Gauss-\(\mu\) × uniform-\(\phi\) measure and the J0 reduction for
   axisymmetric planar patterns are appropriate.

2. **The Gram is Hermitian positive semidefinite by construction.** This is a
   sound way to create a radiative loss operator and preserve non-negative
   radiated power.

3. **Using a fixed \(Z\) and fixed \(S\) while changing the excitation is
   correct.** Scan dependence should arise from the excitation vector through
   \(Z_\mathrm{active}\) or \(Sa\), not by rebuilding the physical antenna
   network for every commanded angle.

4. **The aggregate reflected-power metric is correct for power waves:**

   \[
   \Gamma_P=\frac{\lVert Sa\rVert^2}{\lVert a\rVert^2}.
   \]

   It is a power fraction, not a voltage-wave reflection coefficient.

5. **The real-reference and Kurokawa matrix formulas are algebraically
   coherent.** The current \(T\) maps incident power-wave amplitudes to the
   normalized element-current weights used by the far-field kernel.

6. **A front-hemisphere Gram is sensible for elements over an ideal infinite
   ground plane.** It is not sufficient for a freestanding antenna or a
   finite/substrate-backed ground structure.

## Findings

### [P1] The expected monotonic scan law is not a universal physical law

The premise “projected aperture decreases, therefore active impedance must
rise” mixes two different quantities. Projected aperture primarily governs
available aperture gain. Scan impedance additionally depends on polarization,
the electromagnetic boundary condition, the element current basis, substrate,
ground plane, and Floquet modes. In a spectral model the TE wave impedance
contains a \(1/\cos\theta\)-type factor while the TM branch can contain a
\(\cos\theta\)-type factor.

The scalar Gram already produces \(D/\cos\theta\). Supplying a strongly
broadside \(D\propto\cos^n\theta\) suppresses oblique radiative resistance
faster than the Jacobian raises it when \(n>1\). The observed trend is thus the
model's analytical prediction.

**Action:** treat gain roll-off and mismatch as separate outputs. Do not add a
manual projected-aperture correction to \(Z\). Add TE/TM or calibrated
embedded-element physics if a particular antenna is expected to show rising
scan mismatch.

### [P1] \(P_H\) determines dissipation, not the complete complex impedance

`wasm/src/prad.rs` forms \(P_H\), then `wasm/src/match_s.rs:82-93` copies both
its real and imaginary parts into the object later exposed as \(Z\):

\[
Z \leftarrow 2Z_\mathrm{ref}P_H.
\]

A power Gram determines the Hermitian dissipative operator

\[
R_\mathrm{loss}=\frac{Z+Z^H}{2},
\]

not the stored-energy/reactive operator. Reactance cannot be recovered from a
single-frequency power correlation. For the currently used co-planar,
co-aligned, axisymmetric elements, \(P_H\) is real and this mistake is dormant.
For the planned complex, rotated, or polarized fields, directly treating
\(\operatorname{Im}P_H\) as electrical reactance can violate the symmetry of a
reciprocal physical impedance matrix.

**Action:** keep the Gram as a Hermitian \(R_\mathrm{rad}\) operator. Obtain
\(X\) independently from a physical model or imported broadband data. If a
complex port basis is allowed, explicitly transform the loss operator and
impedance in the same port basis rather than assigning
\(\operatorname{Im}P_H\) to \(X\).

### [P1] The phenomenological \(X(\rho)\) has exactly the wrong structure for the requested effect

`wasm/src/match_s.rs:315-370` adds

\[
X_{pq}=X_\mathrm{nn}(d_\min/\rho_{pq})^\alpha
\]

with one sign for every pair, no diagonal self reactance, no orientation, and
no frequency dependence. For an approximately translational array, equal-sign
mutual terms add most coherently at broadside. Their spatial Fourier transform
therefore tends to create the largest active reactance at broadside and less
reactance at large phase gradients. That directly encourages the reported
reflection trend.

Real mutual reactance normally changes sign and oscillates with electrical
distance; it also depends on element orientation, height, ground/substrate,
polarization, and frequency. The diagonal self reactance is usually at least
as important as an arbitrary off-diagonal tail.

**Action:** replace this control with one of:

- an imported complex \(Z(f)\) or Touchstone model in a documented physical
  port basis;
- a user-supplied radial/orientation kernel
  \(Z(\Delta x,\Delta y,f,\mathrm{orientation})\), including \(Z_\mathrm{self}\);
- a spectral dyadic Green-function model for a chosen elementary radiator and
  ground/substrate stack.

Retain the power-law kernel only as an explicitly labelled qualitative toy.

### [P1] A complex “common Z0” is not an explicit matching network

`wasm/src/match_s.rs:254-312` interprets \(40+j4\ \Omega\), for example, as the
Kurokawa power-wave reference/source impedance. This is mathematically valid,
but changing reference impedance is not the same operation as inserting a
physical matching circuit between a fixed source line and the antenna.

The UI label “Common Z0 match” makes three concepts easy to conflate:

- the real reference impedance used to report an S-matrix;
- the complex source impedance used to define available power waves;
- a realizable, potentially lossy matching network.

**Action:** use a fixed, normally real system reference such as 50 Ω for
reported S-parameters. Represent source impedance and matching networks
explicitly, then cascade/connect their multiport S, Z, or ABCD descriptions.
Tune the network at a declared condition (for example broadside at \(f_0\))
and hold it fixed during scan.

### [P1] The model uses shifted isolated scalar patterns, not physical embedded element patterns

`wasm/src/prad.rs:213-262` and `:331-354` create every element field from the
same scalar \(D(\mu)\) and a position phase. This captures radiative
correlation but omits:

- vector polarization and TE/TM angular wave impedance;
- element orientation and cross-polarized fields;
- edge-dependent embedded patterns;
- ground-plane and dielectric substrate currents;
- surface-wave and evanescent coupling;
- scan blindness and unit-cell resonances;
- frequency variation of self impedance and element pattern.

A high-gain \(\cos^n\) pattern is also selectable independently of lattice cell
area and element geometry. That can describe an unrealizable packing of
isolated apertures even though the Gram remains mathematically passive.

**Action:** support complex vector embedded fields
\((E_\theta,E_\phi)\), normalized to a stated port current or accepted power,
and form

\[
(R_\mathrm{rad})_{pq}\propto
\int \left(E_{\theta,p}^*E_{\theta,q}
E_{\phi,p}^*E_{\phi,q}\right)\,d\Omega.
\]

For a periodic-array mode, a TE/TM Floquet or spectral dyadic model is a
cheaper and more direct way to obtain scan impedance than pretending that a
scalar element factor supplies the missing network physics.

### [P2] Feed illumination is applied after the network

In `js/phasedarray/phasedarray.js:324-353`, \(a\) is built from quantized
taper/phase, then `reflectionRatio(S,a)` and \(w=Ta\) are evaluated. Only
afterward are `vIllumMag` and `vIllumPhaseFactor` multiplied into the
far-field weights. Nonuniform feed illumination therefore does not enter the
incident power, reflected power, accepted power, or coupling transformation.

This is harmless for the default all-ones illumination, but wrong for the
spherical and other nonuniform feed models if they are intended to describe
the actual waves incident on antenna ports.

**Action:** define one physical incident vector including taper, quantization,
manual overrides, and feed illumination. Pass that same vector to both \(S\)
and \(T\). If “illumination” is intended as a downstream aperture-only factor,
rename it and do not use it for port-power claims.

### [P2] The per-port solver does not match a steered array mode

`wasm/src/match_s.rs:110-252` finds diagonal terminations that make each
single-port \(S_{pp}\) small while all other ports have those terminations.
That is a valid simultaneous diagonal-termination problem. It does not make
\(Sa=0\) for broadside or for every scan vector, because off-diagonal
coupling remains.

**Action:** label this mode “simultaneous port termination match.” Add separate
choices for:

- a fixed common network tuned to broadside;
- a fixed per-port network tuned to a declared excitation;
- ideal scan-dependent retuning, clearly marked as nonphysical/upper-bound.

### [P2] The UI lacks the diagnostics needed to distinguish the mechanisms

Only aggregate returned power is exposed. For a chosen current vector \(i\),
the useful antenna diagnostic is

\[
Z_{\mathrm{active},p}=\frac{(Zi)_p}{i_p}.
\]

For incident power waves, the directly observable active reflection is

\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{active},p}=\frac{(Sa)_p}{a_p}.
\]

The aggregate \(\lVert Sa\rVert^2/\lVert a\rVert^2\) can hide severe mismatch
on edge ports or cancellation among scan trends.

**Action:** add scan plots and per-port maps for
\(\Re Z_\mathrm{active}\), \(\Im Z_\mathrm{active}\),
\(|\Gamma_\mathrm{active}|\), and returned-power contribution
\(|b_p|^2\). Also report the collective quadratic resistance
\(i^HRi/i^Hi\), with a warning that it is not a driving-point impedance.

### [P2] Radiation, loss, and accepted power are currently conflated

The Gram contains only the selected radiative hemisphere. There is no
conductor loss, dielectric loss, feed loss, surface-wave loss, or rear
radiation channel. Within that idealized model, accepted power should close to
radiated power. For a physical array, low reflected power can instead mean
power was dissipated or trapped rather than usefully radiated.

**Action:** maintain separate positive-semidefinite operators:

\[
R_\mathrm{total}=R_\mathrm{rad}+R_\mathrm{ohmic}
+R_\mathrm{dielectric}+R_\mathrm{surface/rear}.
\]

Use \(R_\mathrm{total}\) to form \(Z\), but use \(R_\mathrm{rad}\) alone for
radiation efficiency and far-field power.

### [P2] The normalization parameter is overloaded

`wasm/src/prad.rs:277-305` uses `z_ref` to scale the unit-diagonal power Gram
into a 50 Ω self resistance. The same number is then used to normalize \(T\).
This guarantees an isolated element is resonant and 50 Ω, independent of
element kind and frequency. It is a convenient basis calibration, not a
prediction of antenna impedance.

**Action:** separate:

- field/current normalization;
- isolated \(R_\mathrm{rad}(f)\);
- self \(X_\mathrm{self}(f)\);
- loss resistance;
- S-parameter reporting reference;
- source impedance.

### [P2] Tests prove the algebra but not the desired physics

The present tests cover Gram agreement, Hermitian structure, solver residual,
small \(S_{ii}\), and matrix-vector multiplication. They do not test:

- analytical scan laws such as \(D/\cos\theta\);
- active impedance or per-port active reflection versus scan;
- passivity via \(I-S^HS\succeq0\);
- reciprocity in the physical port basis;
- accepted-power versus integrated-radiated-power closure;
- nonuniform illumination through \(S\) and \(T\);
- frequency continuity, grating-lobe onset, or scan blindness.

**Action:** add these tests before interpreting the surrogate as a physical
matching model.

## Recommended implementation path

### Stage 1: make the present surrogate transparent

1. Rename \(P_H\)-derived output to `R_rad`, not generic complex `Z`.
2. Separate the six normalization/source/reference quantities listed above.
3. Add \(Z_\mathrm{active}\), \(\Gamma_\mathrm{active}\), and scan-curve
   diagnostics.
4. Move actual feed illumination into the incident wave vector.
5. Add the analytical \(D/\cos\theta\), passivity, and power-closure tests.
6. Label the current \(X(\rho)\) as phenomenological.

This stage explains the current behavior without claiming to change it.

### Stage 2: add a realistic finite-array data path

1. Accept imported complex \(Z(f)\) or S-parameter data.
2. Validate dimensions, reference impedance, reciprocity, and passivity.
3. Accept embedded element fields using the same physical port basis.
4. Keep \(R_\mathrm{rad}\), other losses, and \(X\) separate.
5. Cascade an explicit matching/feed network.
6. Reuse the existing \(S\), \(T\), far-field, and reflected-power machinery.

This is the shortest path to credible results for a specific antenna.

### Stage 3: add an analytical periodic-array model

For exploratory behavior without full-wave files:

1. choose a radiator model and orientation;
2. use a layered-medium dyadic Green function;
3. decompose into TE/TM Floquet modes;
4. include propagating, evanescent, and surface-wave terms;
5. compute \(Z_\mathrm{active}(\theta,\phi,f)\);
6. optionally inverse-transform sampled scan impedance into a translational
   mutual-\(Z\) kernel for finite arrays.

This naturally produces polarization-dependent scan impedance, grating-lobe
transitions, resonances, and possible scan blindness.

## What not to introduce

- Do not multiply \(R\), \(Z\), or \(S\) by an ad-hoc
  \(1/\cos\theta\) during steering.
- Do not make the antenna matrix itself depend on commanded scan angle unless
  modeling an explicitly reconfigured physical structure.
- Do not infer reactance from a single-frequency power Gram.
- Do not assume a more directive isolated \(\cos^n\) factor must increase
  off-broadside mismatch.
- Do not treat a complex S-parameter reference as a realizable matching
  network.

## Practical answer to the original question

If the desired antenna is expected to show rising reflection at wide scan,
introduce **a physical vector scan-impedance model or calibrated complex
\(Z(f)\)**, then keep a real feed reference and a fixed explicit matching
network. Include TE/TM polarization, self reactance, sign-changing
frequency-dependent mutual reactance, substrate/ground effects, and
surface/Floquet modes as appropriate.

Within the current scalar framework alone, the closest honest improvement is a
user-supplied complex mutual-impedance kernel plus self impedance and active-
impedance plots. Changing \(n\), adding an equal-sign decaying reactance, or
offsetting common \(Z_0\) cannot reliably create the requested physical scan
behavior.
